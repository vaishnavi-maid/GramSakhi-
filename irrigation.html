💧 Irrigation Information

Use Water Wisely, Grow Better Crops

🌱 Importance of Irrigation

Irrigation provides crops with the water they need when rainfall is not sufficient. Proper irrigation can support healthy crop growth, improve water-use efficiency and reduce unnecessary water loss.

💧 What is Irrigation?

Irrigation is the artificial application of water to agricultural land to meet crop water requirements.


The amount and timing of irrigation depend on the crop, soil, weather, crop growth stage, rainfall and available water.

🚜 Main Irrigation Methods

💧 Drip Irrigation

Water is delivered slowly near the root zone through emitters.


Benefits:
  • Can reduce water loss.
  • Delivers water close to roots.
  • Useful for many vegetables and fruit crops.
  • Can be combined with fertigation where appropriate.

🌧️ Sprinkler Irrigation

Water is sprayed over the crop or soil surface like rainfall.


Benefits:
  • Useful for many field and vegetable crops.
  • Can provide relatively uniform water application.
  • Suitable for some light and uneven soils.

🌊 Surface / Flood Irrigation

Water is distributed over the soil surface.


Important:
  • Simple to operate.
  • Can require more water than efficient methods.
  • Careful field leveling and water management can reduce losses.

🌱 Furrow Irrigation

Water flows through small channels between crop rows.


Benefits:
  • Suitable for certain row crops.
  • Keeps much of the plant canopy away from direct water flow.
  • Requires proper field layout and water management.

⏰ When Should Farmers Irrigate?

🌱 Check Soil Moisture

Check the soil near the root zone rather than irrigating only according to a fixed calendar.

🌦️ Consider Rainfall

Reduce or postpone irrigation when sufficient rainfall has already supplied the crop's needs.

🌾 Consider Crop Stage

Water requirements change during germination, vegetative growth, flowering, fruiting and maturity.

🌡️ Consider Weather

Temperature, wind, humidity and rainfall affect how quickly soil loses water.

💧 Water-Saving Tips

  • Prefer efficient irrigation systems such as drip or sprinkler where suitable.
  • Irrigate according to soil moisture and crop requirements.
  • Repair leaking pipes, valves and emitters.
  • Use mulching where appropriate to reduce evaporation.
  • Avoid unnecessary irrigation during or immediately after adequate rainfall.
  • Maintain irrigation equipment regularly.
  • Keep fields properly leveled where surface irrigation is used.
  • Use rainwater and available water resources carefully.

🌧️ Rainwater Harvesting

Rainwater harvesting can help collect and store rainfall for later agricultural use where appropriate.


🏞️ Farm Pond

A properly designed farm pond can store rainwater for later use.

🌱 Field Bunding

Suitable bunding can help slow runoff and support water conservation.

💧 Recharge Structures

Suitable recharge structures can help support groundwater recharge where technically feasible.

🌧️ Rooftop Rainwater

Rainwater from suitable structures can be collected and managed for appropriate uses.

⚠️ Problems Caused by Over-Irrigation

  • Waterlogging
  • Root damage
  • Nutrient losses
  • Higher disease risk in some crops
  • Soil salinity problems in susceptible areas
  • Unnecessary electricity and water costs

🔧 Irrigation System Maintenance

🔍 Check Pipes

Regularly inspect pipes and connections for leakage.

💧 Clean Filters

Clean irrigation filters according to the equipment manufacturer's recommendations.

🚿 Check Emitters

Inspect drip emitters and sprinklers for blockage or uneven discharge.

⚡ Check Pump

Maintain pumps and electrical equipment safely and according to manufacturer instructions.

⚠️ Important:

There is no single irrigation schedule that works for every farm.

The correct amount and timing of irrigation depend on crop, soil type, crop growth stage, rainfall, temperature, irrigation method and water availability.

For crop-specific irrigation scheduling, farmers should consult the local Agriculture Department or a qualified agricultural expert.
