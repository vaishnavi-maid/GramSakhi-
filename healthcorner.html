Important medical notice
This page is for health education and first-aid awareness only. It is not a diagnosis, prescription or substitute for a qualified doctor. Never choose a medicine or dose only from a website. For an emergency in India, call 112 or seek the nearest emergency department.

🚨 Emergency: act quickly

Get emergency help for any of these warning signs:

Breathing / heart
  • Severe difficulty breathing, blue/grey lips, choking
  • Chest pressure/pain, especially with sweating or breathlessness
Brain / injury
  • Sudden face droop, arm weakness, speech difficulty
  • Unconsciousness, seizure, severe bleeding, major injury
Other danger signs
  • Severe allergic reaction, poisoning, serious burns
  • Pregnancy with heavy bleeding or severe pain
  • Baby very sleepy, struggling to breathe or not feeding

👶 Health care at every age

Newborns (0–28 days)

  • Keep the baby warm; practise skin-to-skin care when safe.
  • Support early and exclusive breastfeeding where possible; ask a health worker for feeding support.
  • Attend newborn check-ups and follow the official immunisation schedule.
  • Seek urgent care for fever, low temperature, poor feeding, difficult breathing, convulsions, or unusual sleepiness.

Babies & toddlers

  • Use safe sleep: baby on their back, on a firm flat surface, without loose pillows or blankets.
  • Never shake a baby; keep small objects, medicines and chemicals out of reach.
  • Introduce complementary foods around 6 months while continuing breastfeeding as advised.
  • Never give honey to a child under 12 months; never give medicines without checking with a clinician/pharmacist.

Children & teenagers

  • Balanced meals, clean water, sleep, play and age-appropriate physical activity.
  • Keep vaccinations, dental checks, vision checks and growth monitoring up to date.
  • Talk calmly about puberty, periods, personal safety, online safety and emotional wellbeing.
  • For children, medicine choice and dose depend on age/weight and product strength; never guess a dose.

Adults & working people

  • Aim for regular movement, adequate sleep, nutritious food and tobacco-free living.
  • Check blood pressure, blood sugar and other screening needs with a health professional.
  • Use protective equipment at work and take breaks from prolonged sitting.
  • Take prescribed medicines as directed; do not share prescriptions.

🩹 First aid basics

First aid helps while arranging appropriate care. Make sure the scene is safe; use gloves/barrier if available. Do not put yourself at risk.

Cuts & bleeding

  • Apply firm, continuous pressure with clean cloth or sterile dressing.
  • If blood soaks through, add another layer without removing the first.
  • Call emergency help for heavy, spurting or persistent bleeding, deep wounds or signs of shock.
  • Do not remove deeply embedded objects; apply pressure around them and get urgent help.

Burns / scalds

  • Move away from the heat source and cool the burn under cool running water.
  • Remove jewellery or tight items near the area if not stuck to skin.
  • Cover loosely with a clean non-fluffy dressing and seek medical advice for significant burns.
  • Do not apply ice, toothpaste, oil, ghee, turmeric or creams; do not burst blisters.

Choking

  • If the person can cough and speak, encourage coughing and watch closely.
  • If they cannot breathe, speak or cough effectively, call emergency help and start age-appropriate choking first aid if trained.
  • For infants, use infant-specific first-aid steps; never do blind finger sweeps.

Take an accredited first-aid course to learn practical techniques.

Fainting / unconsciousness

  • Check safety and responsiveness; call 112 if unresponsive or not recovering quickly.
  • Check normal breathing. If not breathing normally, call emergency services and begin CPR/AED if trained, following dispatcher instructions.
  • Do not give food, drink or tablets to an unconscious person.

Sprain, suspected fracture or injury

  • Rest and protect the injured part; keep it still in a comfortable position.
  • Use a wrapped cold pack briefly for swelling if appropriate; never put ice directly on skin.
  • Do not straighten a deformity or push a bone back. Seek urgent care for severe pain, deformity, numbness or inability to use the limb.

Nosebleed

  • Sit upright, lean forward and pinch the soft part of the nose continuously for 10–15 minutes.
  • Breathe through the mouth; do not tilt the head back or lie flat.
  • Seek urgent care if bleeding is heavy, follows a major injury, causes faintness or does not stop.

Poisoning / chemical exposure

  • Call emergency services or seek urgent medical/poison advice immediately.
  • Do not induce vomiting or give home remedies unless a qualified professional instructs you.
  • Keep the container/label for clinicians if safe. For eye/skin chemical exposure, rinse with lots of clean running water while arranging help.

Seizure / fits

  • Protect from nearby hazards, cushion the head and time the seizure.
  • Do not restrain the person and do not put anything in their mouth.
  • Call emergency help for a first seizure, a seizure lasting 5 minutes or more, repeated seizures, injury, pregnancy or breathing difficulty.

🤒 Common health problems: safer first steps

These are general comfort measures, not diagnosis or prescriptions. If symptoms are severe, persistent or worsening, contact a health professional.

Fever

  • Check temperature with a thermometer; rest and drink fluids.
  • For a baby under 3 months with a temperature of 38°C or more, seek urgent medical assessment.
  • Seek urgent help for confusion, stiff neck, breathing trouble, seizure, severe dehydration or a non-fading rash.
  • Do not guess medicine doses, combine products with the same ingredient, or give aspirin to children unless specifically prescribed.

Cold, cough & sore throat

  • Rest, drink fluids and consider warm drinks for comfort.
  • Honey may soothe a cough only for people aged 1 year and older; never for babies under 1.
  • Avoid smoke and unsafe steam bowls that can cause burns.
  • Antibiotics do not treat viral colds. Ask a pharmacist/doctor before cough-and-cold medicines, especially for children, pregnancy or chronic illness.

Headache / body ache

  • Rest, drink water and take a break from screens if that helps.
  • Get emergency help for a sudden worst-ever headache, weakness, confusion, fainting, stiff neck with fever or headache after serious injury.
  • A clinician/pharmacist can advise whether a pain reliever is suitable based on age, pregnancy, other medicines and health conditions.

Stomach upset / diarrhoea

  • Take frequent small sips of safe fluids; oral rehydration solution (ORS) can help replace fluid losses when prepared exactly as directed on the packet.
  • Seek care for blood in stool, severe abdominal pain, repeated vomiting, very little urine, confusion or signs of dehydration.
  • Babies, older adults and people with serious illnesses can become dehydrated quickly.

Allergy / rash

  • Stop exposure to a suspected trigger when safe and ask a professional about mild symptoms.
  • Swelling of tongue/throat, difficulty breathing, collapse or rapidly worsening symptoms are an emergency: call 112.
  • Do not use someone else’s prescription or apply unknown creams to a rash.

Pain, acidity or other symptoms

  • Pain can have many causes; note its location, start time, severity and related symptoms.
  • Chest pain, severe abdominal pain, sudden severe testicular pain, severe eye pain or pain with faintness needs urgent assessment.
  • Avoid taking multiple painkillers or combining cold/flu products without checking ingredients with a pharmacist.
Medicine safety rule

This website intentionally does not provide a symptom-to-medicine prescription chart. Never self-start antibiotics, steroids, sleeping pills, injections or someone else’s prescription. Confirm medicine, dose, timing, interactions and duration with a registered doctor or pharmacist.

🌸 Women’s health

Periods & menstrual health

  • Track cycle dates, bleeding, pain and symptoms.
  • Seek care for very heavy bleeding, faintness, severe pain, bleeding between periods or a major change from usual.
  • Use clean menstrual products and change them regularly; wash hands before and after.

Pregnancy & after birth

  • Book antenatal care early and follow the doctor’s advice on supplements, tests and vaccines.
  • Urgent warning signs include heavy bleeding, severe headache/vision changes, severe abdominal pain, seizures, breathing difficulty or reduced fetal movement as advised by maternity care.
  • After birth, severe bleeding, fever, chest pain, breathlessness or severe headache needs urgent care.

Breast, cervical & reproductive health

  • Ask a qualified health professional about age-appropriate breast and cervical cancer screening and HPV vaccination.
  • Seek assessment for a new breast lump, unusual nipple discharge, persistent pelvic pain or unusual bleeding.
  • Confidential, respectful care is important; seek help for sexual or reproductive health concerns.

🧑 Men’s health

Prevention & routine checks

  • Discuss blood pressure, diabetes, cholesterol, mental health and age/family-history-related screening with a clinician.
  • Avoid tobacco, limit alcohol, stay active and use helmets/seat belts and workplace protection.
  • Seek care for persistent urinary symptoms, a breast/testicular lump, blood in urine or ongoing sexual-health concerns.

Mental health for everyone

  • Talk to someone trusted; sleep, activity and social connection can support wellbeing.
  • Persistent sadness, anxiety, substance use or trouble functioning deserves professional support.
  • If someone may harm themselves or another person, stay with them if safe and get urgent local emergency/professional help.

👵 Senior citizen care

Daily care & independence

  • Support safe movement, balanced meals, hydration, sleep and social connection.
  • Reduce falls: good lighting, non-slip footwear, handrails and clear walking paths.
  • Arrange hearing, vision, dental and mobility checks when needed.

Medicines & chronic conditions

  • Keep an updated list of all medicines, supplements, allergies and treating clinicians.
  • Do not stop or change prescribed medicines without medical advice; ask about side effects and interactions.
  • Know the person’s usual symptoms and seek urgent care for sudden confusion, weakness, chest pain or breathing difficulty.

🧘 Yoga, exercise, food & daily wellbeing

Safe movement

  • Start gently and build up gradually; walking, cycling, dancing and suitable strength/balance work are options.
  • Warm up, use comfortable clothing and drink water. Stop and seek advice for chest pain, faintness or unusual breathlessness.
  • People with pregnancy, recent surgery, heart/lung conditions, severe joint pain or balance problems should ask a clinician which activities are safe.

Yoga & breathing

  • Choose beginner-friendly movements and gentle breathing; never force a stretch or hold breath painfully.
  • Avoid headstands, extreme twists and advanced poses without trained supervision.
  • Yoga can support wellbeing but does not replace prescribed treatment or emergency care.

Nutrition & hydration

  • Eat varied foods: vegetables, fruit, pulses/beans, whole grains, nuts/seeds and suitable protein sources.
  • Use safe drinking water; wash produce and hands; cook and store food safely.
  • Reduce excess salt, sugary drinks and highly processed foods; ask a clinician/dietitian for diabetes, kidney disease or other special diets.

Sleep & hygiene

  • Keep a regular sleep schedule and a calm, dark sleeping space.
  • Wash hands with soap, maintain oral hygiene, ventilate rooms and avoid smoking indoors.
  • Use mosquito prevention where relevant and seek care for persistent sleep or breathing problems.

🛡️ Prevention & health preparedness

Vaccination

Follow the current Government of India immunisation schedule for children, pregnancy and adults. Keep vaccination records and ask an ANM, ASHA worker or doctor about missed doses. Schedules can change, so confirm with an official health worker.

Check-ups & screening

Discuss blood pressure, diabetes, dental/eye care, cancer screening, mental health and vaccinations according to age, risks and family history. Screening needs are individual; a clinician can advise.

Home first-aid kit

  • Clean gauze, sterile dressings, adhesive plasters, medical tape, gloves and bandage.
  • Digital thermometer, clean scissors, tweezers for superficial splinters, and oral rehydration salts.
  • Keep emergency contacts, personal allergies and prescribed medicine list accessible.
  • Check expiry dates; store medicines locked away from children. Do not keep loose/unlabelled tablets or expired medicines.

Personal health record

Keep emergency contact, allergies, chronic conditions, vaccination record and current prescriptions in a secure place. Do not publish personal medical details publicly on the website.
